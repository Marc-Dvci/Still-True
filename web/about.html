How it works

Rules decide. Evidence is shown.

Every verdict comes from code that names its evidence: what the post says, what the source says now, and when it was read. A model is used in one place only: when you ask for new wording, and a suggestion is refused if it adds a number, date, link or hashtag the post did not contain.

The verdicts

  • Still true nothing in the post contradicts the day it goes out.
  • Review a note is added in Buffer; the post stays scheduled.
  • Held the post is moved to drafts with a note, and keeps its original time for release.

The checks

Check Verdict Rule
Weekday and date disagree Review, fix A weekday next to a date that falls on another day.
Relative day and date disagree Review, fix "Tomorrow" read on the publish day contradicts a date or weekday in the same sentence.
Post moved to another day Review The due date moved since Still True first saw the post, and the post says "today", "tomorrow", "tonight" or "this weekend".
Time already passed Review "Tonight at 7pm" going out after 7pm the same day.
Date already passed Hold Future or promotional wording ("join us", "until", "last day") about a date before the publish date.
Greeting does not fit the hour Review "Good morning" from 18:00, "good night" before noon.
Link is broken Hold The link answers 404 or 410, or 5xx twice in a row.
Link lands on the home page Review A deep link now redirects to the site's root.
Out of stock Hold The product's structured data says OutOfStock, SoldOut or Discontinued.
Price changed Review, fix The post states a price no current offer on the page matches.
Event cancelled or postponed Hold The event's eventStatus says so.
Event date changed Review, fix The event's startDate differs from the date the post states.
The forecast says otherwise Review, fix Sun or outdoor wording into rain (60 % or more, or a wet WMO code); the next dry, bright slot is proposed.
Promotion on a day of remembrance Review A promotional or playful post on a national day of remembrance of the audience's country.
Wording echoes an active disaster Hold A red or orange GDACS alert in the audience's country began in the last 72 hours, and the post uses words tied to that hazard ("on fire" during a wildfire).
Promotion during a disaster Review A red GDACS alert in the audience's country in the last 72 hours, and promotional wording.
Promotion on a day of an attack or disaster Review Wikipedia's Current Events names the audience's country under an attack or disaster that day or the day before.

Supportive posts ("our thoughts are with", "collecting donations", "we will be closed") are never flagged by the disaster and news checks.

When it checks

A sweep every three hours (every thirty minutes when something is due within three hours), then a re-check 60 and 10 minutes before each post goes out. Disaster alerts and news are read once for everyone every thirty minutes, so the load on those services does not grow with the number of users. Each check costs one Buffer request per organisation, plus one write only when something changes.

For agents

Still True also answers AI agents through MCP at /mcp with one tool, check_post. An agent that drafts posts for Buffer can ask before it schedules.

Data sources

  • Disaster alerts: GDACS, the Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System (European Commission and United Nations). Its alerts are used to hold a post for a human, never to decide anything else.
  • News: Wikipedia's Current Events portal, licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Weather: Weather data by Open-Meteo.com, licensed under CC BY 4.0.
  • Remembrance days: a calendar of 31 days in 17 countries, each entry with its official or reference source, in the source code.
  • Product and event facts: the structured data (schema.org) published by the linked page itself.
